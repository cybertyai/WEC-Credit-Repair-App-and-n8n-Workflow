EQUAL CREDIT OPPORTUNITY ACT — Key Provisions Summary
15 U.S.C. §§ 1691-1691f

§ 1691. Scope of prohibition

Creditors are prohibited from discriminating against any applicant regarding any aspect of a credit transaction based on race, color, religion, national origin, sex, marital status, age (provided the applicant has the capacity to contract), because the applicant receives income from a public assistance program, or because the applicant has in good faith exercised any right under the Consumer Credit Protection Act.

§ 1691a. Definitions

An "applicant" is any person who applies to a creditor directly for an extension, renewal, or continuation of credit, or who applies through an intermediary. A "creditor" means any person who regularly extends, renews, or continues credit; any person who regularly arranges for the extension of credit; or any assignee of an original creditor who participates in the decision to extend credit. "Credit" means the right granted by a creditor to defer payment of debt or to incur debt and defer payment.

§ 1691b. Regulations

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau is authorized to prescribe regulations to carry out the purposes of the Act. These regulations may contain exemptions from ECOA requirements where they are not necessary to carry out the purposes of the Act.

§ 1691c. Administrative enforcement

Compliance with ECOA is enforced by: the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency for national banks; the Federal Reserve Board for state member banks; the FDIC for insured nonmember banks; and the CFPB for any creditor not covered by the above. Each agency enforces ECOA through its general supervisory authority over the entities within its jurisdiction.

§ 1691d. Adverse action — notice requirements

Within 30 days of receiving a completed application for credit, a creditor must notify the applicant of its action on the application. If the creditor takes adverse action, it must give the applicant a written statement of the reasons for the adverse action or a written disclosure of the applicant's right to request the reasons within 60 days of notification.

Adverse action includes a denial or revocation of credit, a change in the terms of an existing credit arrangement, or a refusal to grant credit in substantially the amount or terms requested. A statement of reasons must be specific enough for the applicant to understand the particular reasons for the adverse action taken.

§ 1691e. Civil liability

Any creditor who fails to comply with any requirement imposed by ECOA is liable to the aggrieved applicant for: actual damages; punitive damages up to $10,000 in individual actions (up to $500,000 or one percent of the creditor's net worth in class actions); and costs and reasonable attorney fees. An action must be brought within two years of the date of the violation.

In determining the amount of punitive damages, the court considers the amount of any actual damages awarded, the frequency and persistence of failures to comply, the resources of the creditor, the number of persons adversely affected, and the extent to which the creditor's failure was intentional.
